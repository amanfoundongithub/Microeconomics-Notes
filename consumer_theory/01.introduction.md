# Consumer Theory

One of the most interesting and important theory of microeconomics is called `consumer theory`. This theory is a systematic analysis of one of the agents in economics, namely the `consumers`.

As we know, in an economic market, we know that there are two participants, which are producers and consumers.

While the interplay of these two participants is crucial to the economic understanding, it would often make our study easier if we try to keep the other participants constant (_ceteris paribus_) and focus on how the market behaves when one of the participants changes.

Thus, in the consumer theory, we focus on how the market behaves when the consumer's are affected, _ceteris paribus_.

## Who are Consumers?

The first and the foremost question when studying the consumer theory is,

_Who are the consumers we will study?_

To answer this question, we will consider a specific market, for example, a market for noodles.

In a market of noodles, our model assumes two types of participants in the market:

- The participants who will supply the market with the noodles to be sold; these participants are called **producers** in the market.

- The participants who will receive the noodles supplied and demand for the same; these participants are called **consumers** in the market.

Thus, we see that the **consumers are those who are at the receiving end of the market.** They will be the ones who decide whether to buy the product or not.

- Thus, we see that each market has a different set of consumers it aims to sell the goods to.

- Consumers are provided with a choice to buy a good or not; some consumers will be there who will not buy the product.

Armed with this definition, we can now easily delve into the realm of the consumer theory!
